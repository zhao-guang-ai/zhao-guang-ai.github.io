A fractional CMO in Singapore costs between S$7,000 and S$18,000 a month, depending on how many days a month you buy, or roughly S$1,800–3,000 per day. A typical engagement — six days a month for six to twelve months — lands at S$10,000–13,000 a month, which annualises to S$120,000–156,000.

What follows is how those bands are built, what moves a quote, and how to tell a fair proposal from an expensive one.

*All figures are indicative 2026 market ranges for Singapore, not a quotation. Treat them as a benchmark to hold your own offers against.*

## What a fractional CMO costs in Singapore

Pricing is set per day, then converted to a monthly retainer:

- **4 days a month — S$7,000–9,000.** Strategy only: positioning, channel plan, board reporting. Works when you already have a capable marketing lead who executes. Below this, you are buying advice, not ownership.
- **6 days a month — S$10,000–13,000.** The most common structure, and what I would recommend for most Series A and B companies. One recurring leadership meeting, one deep working session, and enough asynchronous review to keep the team moving.
- **8 days a month (two days a week) — S$14,000–18,000.** Full ownership of the function: leading the team, running hiring, owning board reporting, managing agencies. For companies where marketing is the main growth constraint.
- **Day rate for project work — S$1,800–3,000.** Used for GTM sprints, pricing exercises or a one-off audit, usually quoted as a fixed fee for six to ten weeks.

Annualised, the six-day structure is S$120,000–156,000. Against a full-time CMO in Singapore at S$380,000–480,000 fully loaded, that is 30–38% of the cost for the same seniority — the single most useful number in this article.

## What actually drives the price

When two quotes for the same company differ by 40%, it is rarely greed. It is usually one of these:

- **Days per month.** The dominant variable. Everything else is a rounding error next to it.
- **Scope of ownership.** Strategy only sits at the bottom; strategy plus leading your team, hiring, board reporting and managing agencies sits at the top. A provider quoting S$8,000 for what is really an eight-day-a-month job is telling you which parts they plan to skip.
- **Number of markets and languages.** One market in English is the baseline. Singapore plus Indonesia plus Vietnam, with localised content and partners, adds real work — often 20–30% — and should be priced explicitly rather than absorbed silently.
- **Whether they are on the ground.** Monthly flights from another country sound cheap until you need someone in a room in Jakarta next week. Regional travel should be billed separately and transparently.
- **Revenue accountability.** Someone willing to be measured on cost per qualified opportunity and pilot-to-paid conversion is taking career risk a strategy-only advisor is not.

The rule of thumb: you are paying for decision-making capacity, not output volume. If a quote is framed around the number of assets produced, you are being sold execution at strategy prices.

## Fractional CMO vs the alternatives

Run the arithmetic on all four before you decide. Most companies compare two and guess at the rest.

- **Full-time CMO — S$380,000–480,000 fully loaded per year.** Base of S$260,000–320,000 plus CPF, bonus, equity and an amortised recruiter fee. Add four to six months of search once you start, and the twelve months before that where nobody owned marketing. The right end state for many companies — just usually not yet.
- **Marketing agency — S$8,000–25,000 a month, or S$5,000–15,000 per project.** Cheaper per unit of output, and genuinely useful when the brief already exists. An agency does not write the brief, manage your team, or carry your number. If you cannot say what the brief should contain, you are about to spend good money finding out.
- **Junior in-house hire — S$70,000–120,000 a year.** Looks cheapest and is often the most expensive: you still manage them, and if nobody can judge marketing work, you have bought output you cannot evaluate.
- **Fractional CMO at six days a month — S$120,000–156,000 a year.** Senior decisions, part-time presence, and a team that keeps running after the engagement.

The combination that wins most often: a fractional CMO at six days a month plus an agency at S$10,000–15,000 a month. That is roughly S$250,000–330,000 a year for senior ownership *and* production capacity — still below a single full-time CMO, and the agency stops being unmanaged. ## What is usually not included

The retainer buys time and judgement. It does not buy the following, and proposals that blur this line cause the arguments later:

- **Media budget.** Paid spend is yours and sits outside the retainer. If a provider offers to "include" media, check whose money is funding it.
- **Tooling and licences.** CRM seats, marketing automation, SEO tooling and analytics typically run S$1,000–3,000 a month at Series A scale.
- **Production.** Design, video, copywriting volume, event booths — an agency or contractor budget, separate from the retainer. Fractional designers, developers and marketing-ops engineers bill at their own rates too.
- **The hidden cost nobody quotes: your own time.** Expect the founder or a direct report to spend two to four hours a week in working sessions, reviews and decisions. An engagement where nobody on your side has time produces documents.

## How to read a quote

Six things to check before signing, and the signals that should end the conversation:

- **Is the day count stated, and is it per month or per engagement?** "Flexible support" is not a scope. Eight days a month is.
- **Are the deliverables named?** Positioning document, channel plan with budget allocation and expected cost per qualified opportunity, hiring scorecards, monthly board pack. If they cannot be listed, they will not arrive.
- **Who owns the marketing team?** Ask directly whether your marketers take direction from this person. If the answer is "we will advise them", you are buying a consultant at CMO prices.
- **What happens in month one?** A named diagnostic output with a date attached.
- **Is travel, GST and tooling inside or outside the number?** Get it in writing.

The red flags: no named deliverables; "strategy" with no committed hours; pay-for-performance-only deals with no base; exclusivity clauses that stop you hiring your own CMO later; and any quote 40% below the market band — at that price someone senior is not doing the work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a fractional CMO really cheaper than hiring full-time?

At the cash level, yes: S$120,000–156,000 a year versus S$380,000–480,000 fully loaded, plus four to six months of search. Not in every case — if you genuinely need someone in the building five days a week, hire full-time. Fractional wins when you need senior decisions more than senior presence.

### Can I get a fractional CMO for two days a month?

You can, at roughly S$4,000–5,000 a month, and you should be sceptical about what it delivers. Two days a month is enough to review and advise, not enough to lead a team and change a trajectory. If the budget only stretches that far, spend it on a positioning sprint instead.

### Is there a minimum engagement?

Three months, and six is better. Anything shorter than a quarter produces a diagnosis without the chance to act on it. Most engagements run six to twelve months, with a review at month three and a handover plan agreed from the start.

### Do you work with companies below Series A?

Occasionally, when there is a paying product and at least one repeatable sales motion. Below roughly S$1M ARR without either, the money is better spent on customer interviews. The reasoning is in [what a fractional CMO is](/insights/what-is-a-fractional-cmo).
